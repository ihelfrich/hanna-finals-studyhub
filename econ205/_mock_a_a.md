## Section II: Short Answer

### 21) Stagflation
1. SRAS shifts left (cost shock). AD unchanged in initial impact.
2. P↑, Y↓, u↑ — stagflation. (Intuition: along stable AD, leftward SRAS gives higher P and lower Y.)
3. Easing fights output loss but worsens inflation; tightening fights inflation but deepens recession. The supply shock creates a policy trade-off impossible under demand shocks. Most central banks prioritize inflation expectations to avoid de-anchoring.

### 22) Fisher
1. $r \approx 4.5\% - 2.5\% = 2.0\%$.
2. $1+r = 1.045/1.025 = 1.01951$ → r = 1.95%.
3. Realized π (4%) > expected (2.5%). Borrower (homeowner) gains: real cost of debt falls. Lender (bank) loses: real return falls. Intuition: unexpected inflation transfers wealth from creditors to debtors.

### 23) Money multiplier
1. $1/0.10 = 10$.
2. $\Delta M = 10 \times 50 = \$500$ million.
3. (a) Banks hold excess reserves (especially when IOR > market rate). (b) Public holds some cash, reducing redeposits. Either dampens the multiplier.

### 24) Comparative advantage
1. Country A: opp cost of 1 wheat = 3/6 = 0.5 cloth. Country B: opp cost of 1 wheat = 4/4 = 1 cloth.
2. A has CA in wheat (lower opp cost). B has CA in cloth.
3. Trade is mutually beneficial when 0.5 < (cloth per wheat) < 1.0. Any ratio in (0.5, 1.0) works (e.g., 0.75 cloth per wheat).
   Intuition: terms must lie strictly between the two domestic opportunity-cost ratios.

### 25) Crowding out vs crowding in
- Crowding out: G↑ (deficit-financed) raises loanable-funds demand → r↑ → private I↓ (and NX↓). Offsets stimulus.
- Crowding in: when slack is severe (deep recession, ZLB), G↑ raises Y, expectations, and capacity utilization without pushing r up; private firms see higher demand and invest. Mechanism: accelerator effect on I dominates, monetary policy accommodates so r stays low.

## Section III: Quantitative Problems

### 26) Multiplier
1. Output gap = Y - Y* = 18,000 - 19,000 = -1,000 (negative gap, recessionary).
2. $1/(1-0.8)=5$.
3. $\Delta G = 1000/5 = \$200$.
4. Tax multiplier = $-0.8/0.2 = -4$. To raise Y by 1,000: $\Delta T = -1000/(-4) = -250$ (a $250 tax cut).
5. If 30% offset, effective multiplier = $5 \times 0.7 = 3.5$. Required $\Delta G = 1000/3.5 \approx \$285.7$.

### 27) Quantity theory

2. $\pi = \%\Delta M - \%\Delta Y = 7-3 = 4\%$.
3. Need $\%\Delta M = \pi + \%\Delta Y = 2 + 3 = 5\%$.

### 28) Tariff (parameters fixed per Gemini review — see note in question)

**Step 0 — autarky check (always do this first):** $Q^D = Q^S \Rightarrow 120 - 2P = 2P \Rightarrow P_A = 30$. Since $P_w = 20 < P_A$, country is an importer. After tariff, price rises to $25$, still below $P_A = 30$, so imports remain positive — tariff is *not* prohibitive.

1. Free-trade at $P = 20$: $Q^D = 120 - 40 = 80$, $Q^S = 40$, imports = $40$.
2. Post-tariff at $P = 25$: $Q^D = 120 - 50 = 70$, $Q^S = 50$, imports = $20$.
3. Tariff revenue = tariff × imports = $\$5 × 20 = \$100$.
4. Deadweight loss — two triangles with the price wedge of $5:
   - Production-side: $\frac{1}{2} × (Q^S\text{-rise} = 10) × \$5 = \$25$.
   - Consumption-side: $\frac{1}{2} × (Q^D\text{-fall} = 10) × \$5 = \$25$.
   - **Total DWL = \$50.**

   Intuition: tariff transfers consumer surplus to producers and the government, with the two triangles representing pure welfare loss from distorted production (inefficient domestic suppliers replacing cheaper imports) and lost consumption.

::: {.callout-tip}
**Why this is now a good problem (Gemini-fix):** the autarky price ($30$) is strictly above the post-tariff price ($25$), so imports stay positive, tariff revenue is non-zero, and all four welfare components (CS loss, PS gain, govt revenue, DWL) are non-trivial. The original parameters made the tariff prohibitive — a degenerate edge case Pérez is unlikely to test.
:::

### 29) International finance
1. $NX = S-I = 2500-2800 = -300$ (deficit of $300B).
2. CA<0 → KA>0 (capital inflow of $300B).
3. r↑ → capital inflow → $ appreciates → X↓, M↑, NX↓ (further into deficit).
4. PPP: $E_{long-run} = P^*/P$. Higher US π means US prices rise faster → dollar must depreciate long run (about 3% per year against the foreign currency to preserve PPP).

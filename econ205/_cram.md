# ECON 205 Final Exam One-Page Cram Sheet

Cumulative — Lectures 01–19. Heavy weight on Ch. 11–19 (post-midterm). Memorize before exam day; only pencil + calculator allowed.

## 1) Pre-Midterm Refresher (Ch. 3–9)
- Spending GDP: $Y = C + I + G + NX$, $NX = X - M$
- Saving: $S_p = Y - C - T$, $S_g = T - G$, $S = Y - C - G$, open economy $S = I + NX$
- GDP deflator: $\text{Def} = \frac{\text{Nominal}}{\text{Real}} \times 100$; inflation $\pi = \frac{\text{Def}_t-\text{Def}_{t-1}}{\text{Def}_{t-1}}$
- Labor: $\text{LF}=E+U$, $u=U/\text{LF}$, $\text{LFPR}=\text{LF}/\text{adults}$, $\text{EPOP}=E/\text{adults}$
- Production: $Y=AK^{0.5}L^{0.5}$ → K alone doubles → $Y \times \sqrt{2}$ (DMR); both double → $Y \times 2$ (CRS)
- Midpoint elasticity uses average of endpoints in denominator. Elastic ($|\varepsilon|>1$): P↑ → revenue↓.

## 2) Money & Inflation (Ch. 11)
- Quantity equation: $M \cdot V = P \cdot Y$
- Quantity theory (V, Y stable in LR): $\%\Delta M \approx \pi$
- Fisher equation (exact): $1+i = (1+r)(1+\pi^e)$; approx: $i \approx r + \pi^e$
- Real rate: $r \approx i - \pi^e$
- Costs of inflation: shoe-leather, menu, tax distortions, wealth redistribution from creditors to debtors when unexpected
- Hyperinflation typically caused by monetization of fiscal deficits

## 3) Fluctuations & AD/AS (Ch. 12–14)
- Okun's Law (rule of thumb): $\Delta u \approx -0.5 \cdot (\text{real GDP gap growth})$
- AD slopes down: wealth, interest-rate, exchange-rate effects
- SRAS slopes up (sticky wages/prices); LRAS vertical at $Y^*$ (potential)
- Demand shock: P and Y move same direction (e.g., AD↓ → recession with disinflation)
- Supply shock: P and Y move opposite (e.g., SRAS↓ → stagflation: P↑, Y↓, u↑)
- Long-run self-correction: prices/wages adjust → SRAS shifts to restore $Y^*$

## 4) Fiscal Policy (Ch. 15)
- Spending multiplier (closed, no taxes): $\frac{1}{1-MPC}$
- Tax multiplier: $\frac{-MPC}{1-MPC}$
- Balanced-budget multiplier $= 1$
- Open-economy multiplier shrinks: $\frac{1}{1-MPC(1-t)+m}$ (m = marginal propensity to import)
- Crowding out: G↑ → r↑ → I↓ (and NX↓ via currency appreciation)
- Automatic stabilizers: progressive taxes, unemployment insurance — dampen cycles without legislation
- Deficit = G + Transfers − T (flow); Debt = stock

## 5) Monetary Policy (Ch. 16)
- Fed tools: open-market operations, IOR (interest on reserves), discount rate, reserve requirement
- Expansionary: buy bonds → reserves↑ → fed funds rate↓ → r↓ → I↑, C↑, NX↑ (via $) → AD right
- Taylor Rule (illustrative): $i = r^* + \pi + 0.5(\pi - \pi^*) + 0.5(y - y^*)$
- Money multiplier (simple): $1/rr$; broader $M = mm \cdot MB$
- Lags: recognition, decision, implementation, impact (~6–18 mo)
- Zero Lower Bound → QE, forward guidance

## 6) Financial Markets (Ch. 17)
- Bond price and yield move inversely
- Present value: $PV = \sum \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t}$
- Higher r → lower bond/asset prices, lower I demand
- Risk premium = required return − risk-free rate
- Loanable-funds: S (savers) ↑ in r, D (borrowers) ↓ in r; deficit→S left→r↑→crowding out

## 7) International Trade (Ch. 18)
- Comparative advantage: lowest opportunity cost (NOT lowest absolute cost)
- Both nations gain by specializing in their CA good and trading
- Terms of trade must lie between the two opportunity-cost ratios
- Tariff: domestic P↑, Q domestic↑, Q imports↓, deadweight loss = two triangles, gov gets tariff revenue rectangle
- Quota: similar to tariff but rent goes to license holders, not government

## 8) International Finance (Ch. 19)
- Nominal exchange rate $E$ = foreign currency per $1 (or vice versa — define!)
- $ appreciates → US exports more expensive abroad → X↓, M↑ → NX↓
- Real exchange rate: $\varepsilon = E \cdot P/P^*$
- PPP (long run): $E = P^*/P$
- Interest-rate parity: capital flows to higher-r country → currency appreciates
- BoP: Current Account + Capital/Financial Account ≈ 0
- $S - I = NX$ (capital outflow = trade surplus)

## 9) Top Final-Exam Traps
1. Real vs nominal interest rate — Fisher direction
2. AD shock vs SRAS shock — co-movement of P and Y
3. Crowding out missed when discussing fiscal expansion
4. Comparative advantage based on absolute cost
5. Exchange-rate direction: appreciation HURTS exports
6. Quantity theory: confusing $\%\Delta M$ with $\Delta M$
7. Tax multiplier sign and magnitude (smaller than spending multiplier)
8. Forgetting LRAS is vertical → no long-run output effect of AD shifts
9. Open-economy multiplier: imports leak → smaller multiplier
10. Bond price ↔ yield inverse relationship

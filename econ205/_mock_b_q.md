2. Identify two distortions imposed by hyperinflation on the economy. (3)
3. State the usual stabilization package required. (2)

### 22) AD/AS diagrammatic short-answer (8 pts)

1. Which curve shifts and in which direction? (2)
2. Predict short-run effects on P, Y, and u. (3)
3. Trace the long-run adjustment if no policy is taken. (3)

### 23) Loanable funds and policy (8 pts)

1. Which curve shifts in loanable funds and how? (3)

1. Compute the implied yield. (3)
2. If market r rises to 7%, the new price (assuming the bond hasn't matured)? (3)

## Section III: Quantitative Problems (50 points)

### 26) Open-economy multiplier and crowding out (16 pts)
MPC = 0.7, marginal propensity to import m = 0.15, no income tax. Y = 22,000, Y* = 24,000.

1. Closed-economy multiplier vs simple open-economy multiplier $1/(1-MPC+m)$. (4)
2. Output gap and required ΔG using open-economy multiplier (no crowding out). (4)
3. Tax-cut version: required ΔT to close gap. (4)
4. If crowding out absorbs 25% of stimulus, recompute required ΔG. (4)

### 27) Inflation arithmetic (10 pts)
CPI rises from 280 to 294 over a year.

1. Compute inflation rate. (3)
2. If your nominal wage rose from $50,000 to $51,500, did your real wage rise or fall? Show the computation. (4)
3. If a CD pays 6% nominal and inflation is the rate from #1, compute the approximate and exact real returns. (3)

### 28) Comparative advantage with PPF (12 pts)
Two countries, two goods. Each country has 100 hours of labor.

| | Hours per Cars | Hours per Computers |
|---|---:|---:|
| Country A | 4 | 5 |
| Country B | 6 | 3 |

1. Compute max output of each good in each country in autarky. (2)
2. Compute opportunity cost of each good in each country. (4)
3. Identify each country's CA. (2)
4. State the terms-of-trade range supporting mutual gains. (2)
5. If countries fully specialize and trade at 1 car = 1 computer, name one country that strictly prefers this to autarky and why. (2)

### 29) Monetary policy transmission (12 pts)

1. Trace effects on long-term r, I, C, NX, and AD in five short steps. (6)
2. Predict short-run effects on P, Y, u. (3)
3. If the economy is at full employment, predict long-run effects per the AD/AS model. (3)

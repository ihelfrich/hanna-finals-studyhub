## Section I: Multiple Choice (40 points; 2 each, 20 questions)

1. If the Fed buys government bonds in the open market, the most direct effect on the federal funds rate is to:
A. Raise it  B. Lower it  C. Leave it unchanged  D. Make it more volatile

2. According to the quantity equation $MV=PY$, if M grows 8%, V is constant, and real GDP grows 2%, predicted inflation is approximately:
A. 2%  B. 6%  C. 8%  D. 10%

3. Using the Fisher approximation, if i = 5% and π^e = 3%, the real rate equals:
A. 8%  B. 2%  C. 1.94%  D. -2%

4. An adverse supply shock in the AD/AS model causes in the short run:
A. P↓, Y↑  B. P↑, Y↑  C. P↑, Y↓  D. P↓, Y↓

5. If MPC = 0.75, the closed-economy spending multiplier equals:
A. 1.33  B. 3.0  C. 4.0  D. 5.0

6. The corresponding tax multiplier (same MPC) equals:
A. -3  B. -4  C. -0.75  D. -0.25

7. A government runs a larger deficit. In the loanable-funds market the predicted effect is:
A. r falls, I rises  B. r rises, I falls  C. r unchanged  D. S shifts right

8. Country A has a comparative advantage in good X if:
A. A produces X with fewer inputs than B  B. A has lower opportunity cost of X than B  C. A consumes more X  D. A has a fixed exchange rate

9. If the US dollar appreciates against the euro, US net exports tend to:
A. Rise  B. Fall  C. Stay the same  D. Become zero

10. Bond prices and bond yields are:
A. Positively related  B. Inversely related  C. Unrelated  D. Equal

11. Forward guidance refers to:
A. Pre-announced fiscal stimulus  B. Central-bank communication about future policy  C. Reserve requirement changes  D. Bond defaults

12. Crowding out occurs because expansionary fiscal policy tends to:
A. Lower r and raise I  B. Raise r and lower I  C. Lower G and raise T  D. Lower domestic prices

13. Automatic stabilizers do NOT include:
A. Progressive income tax  B. Unemployment insurance  C. New legislation passed in a recession  D. Means-tested transfers

14. A vertical LRAS curve implies:
A. Money is non-neutral in the long run  B. Long-run output is independent of P  C. Inflation cannot occur  D. Wages are rigid

15. In a small open economy, an import tariff causes:
A. Lower domestic P, higher imports  B. Higher domestic P, lower imports  C. Lower P, no DWL  D. Higher P, higher imports

16. Purchasing-power parity predicts that in the long run:
A. Exchange rates are random  B. Prices of identical baskets equate after currency conversion  C. r is the same everywhere  D. Tariffs disappear

17. If S = 1200, I = 1500 in an open economy, then NX equals:
A. +300  B. -300  C. 0  D. +1500

18. Unexpected inflation tends to:
A. Help creditors, hurt debtors  B. Help debtors, hurt creditors  C. Help both  D. Hurt both equally

19. In the AD/AS model, long-run self-correction after a negative AD shock works through:
A. SRAS shifting right as wages fall  B. AD shifting right automatically  C. LRAS shifting left  D. Tax cuts only

20. A central bank facing the Zero Lower Bound is most likely to use:
A. Higher reserve requirements  B. Quantitative easing  C. Higher discount rate  D. Selling government bonds

## Section II: Short Answer / Conceptual (40 points; 8 each, 5 questions)

### 21) Stagflation episode (8 pts)
A sharp rise in global oil prices hits the US economy.
1. Which curve(s) shift, and in which direction? (2)
2. Predict short-run effects on P, Y, and u. (3)
3. Should the central bank ease or tighten? Why is the policy choice difficult? (3)

### 22) Fisher and the real rate (8 pts)
The 1-year Treasury yields 4.5% nominal. Surveyed expected inflation is 2.5%.
1. Compute the approximate real rate. (2)
2. Compute the exact real rate via $1+i=(1+r)(1+\pi^e)$. (3)
3. If realized inflation turns out to be 4%, who gains and who loses on a fixed-rate mortgage signed at 4.5%? (3)

### 23) Money multiplier (8 pts)
Banks face a reserve ratio of 10%. The Fed buys $50 million of bonds from a primary dealer.
1. Compute the simple money multiplier. (2)
2. Maximum increase in the money supply if banks hold no excess reserves and the public holds no cash. (3)
3. Give two realistic reasons the actual increase is smaller. (3)

### 24) Comparative advantage (8 pts)
Two countries, two goods. Per worker-hour:

| | Wheat | Cloth |
|---|---:|---:|
| Country A | 6 | 3 |
| Country B | 4 | 4 |

1. Compute opportunity cost of wheat in each country. (3)
2. Identify each country's comparative advantage. (2)
3. State a terms-of-trade range that allows mutually beneficial trade. (3)

### 25) Crowding out vs crowding in (8 pts)
Distinguish crowding out from crowding in. Under what economic conditions might fiscal expansion crowd in private investment? Provide a brief mechanism (4 + 4).

## Section III: Quantitative Problems (50 points)

### 26) AD/AS and fiscal multiplier (16 pts)
Closed economy. MPC = 0.8, no income tax. Current Y = 18,000; potential Y* = 19,000.

1. Output gap (sign and size). (2)
2. Spending multiplier. (3)
3. Required ΔG to close the gap (no crowding out assumed). (3)
4. Tax multiplier and required ΔT (cut) to close the gap. (4)
5. If 30% of stimulus is offset by crowding out, recompute required ΔG. (4)

### 27) Quantity theory and seigniorage (10 pts)
Country has nominal GDP $4 trillion, M = $1 trillion.
1. Compute velocity. (2)
2. If real GDP grows 3% and M grows 7%, V constant — implied inflation. (4)
3. If the central bank targets 2% inflation under same V and growth, what M growth rate is consistent? (4)

### 28) Tariff welfare (12 pts)
Small open economy, world price P_w = $20. Domestic D: $Q^D = 100 - 2P$; Domestic S: $Q^S = 2P$. Government imposes tariff t = $5 (so domestic price under tariff = $25).

1. Free-trade quantities: domestic Q^D, Q^S, imports. (3)
2. Post-tariff quantities. (3)
3. Tariff revenue. (2)
4. Total deadweight loss (sum of two triangles). (4)

### 29) International finance (12 pts)
US: $S$ = 2,500, $I$ = 2,800 (billions).

1. Compute NX. (2)
2. Sign of capital account. (2)
3. If a fiscal expansion raises domestic r, predict the direction of: $ exchange rate, X, M, NX. (4)
4. If long-run PPP holds and US π = 5% while foreign π = 2%, in what direction does the dollar move long run? (4)

## Section IV: Long Applied Problem (20 points)

### 30) Recession response — full transmission
The economy enters a recession: u = 7% (above natural rate of 4.5%), inflation has fallen to 1.5%, and the federal funds rate is at 0.25% (effectively at the ZLB).

a) Sketch (verbally) the AD/AS diagram and identify the type of shock most consistent with this picture. (4)

b) The Fed announces a $1 trillion QE program. Trace the transmission to AD in at least four steps. (5)

c) Congress simultaneously passes a fiscal stimulus of $400 billion in spending. Given MPC = 0.7 and a tax leakage parameter that effectively makes the multiplier 2.0, compute the projected ΔY. (4)

d) Explain why the combined policy package may produce a larger Y response than at normal interest rates (mention crowding out and ZLB). (4)

e) Identify one risk the policy mix creates over the medium term. (3)

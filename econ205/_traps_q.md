# ECON 205 Final Exam — Killer Traps Drill Pack

25 trap-style problems targeting high-frequency student errors on post-midterm material. Each problem isolates a specific failure mode. Time allotted: 1–4 minutes per item.

---

## A) Real vs Nominal Interest / Fisher (5)

1. Nominal i = 8%. Expected inflation = 5%. Realized inflation = 7%. What is the **ex post real** rate? Did the lender win or lose vs expectations?

2. A 1-year loan at 4% nominal. Inflation realized at 4%. Is this loan profitable in real terms for the lender?

3. Use exact Fisher: i = 10%, π^e = 6%. Compute exact r. Then compare to the approximation. Why is the approximation worse at high inflation?

4. A retiree holds a 30-year fixed-rate Treasury bond purchased at 3% nominal. Inflation suddenly rises permanently to 5%. What happens to the bond's market price and the holder's real return?

5. Trap: a student writes "$r = i \cdot \pi^e$." Identify and correct the error.

---

## B) AD/AS Shifts — especially supply shocks (4)

6. A new productivity-enhancing technology spreads through the economy. Which curves shift, and what are the SR and LR predictions for P and Y?

7. Wages negotiated last year exceed expectations of current inflation; firms now face higher real labor costs. Which curve shifts and how? SR predictions for P and Y?

8. The dollar appreciates due to capital inflows. Trace effects on AD components and the AD curve.

9. Trap: A drop in oil prices is wrongly described as an AD shift. Identify the correct curve, direction, and short-run effect.

---

## C) Crowding Out vs Crowding In (3)

10. Government raises G by $200B, deficit-financed. The Fed maintains a strict inflation target and raises r in response. Trace the effect on private I and conclude whether the multiplier exceeds or falls short of the closed-economy textbook value.

11. Same fiscal expansion at the ZLB. The Fed cannot raise r and signals long-term accommodation. What happens to crowding out, and how is the realized multiplier affected?

12. Trap: A student claims tax cuts always crowd in investment. State the conditions under which crowding out still occurs after a tax cut.

---

## D) Open vs Closed Economy Multipliers (3)

13. MPC = 0.8, m = 0. Compute the multiplier. Now set m = 0.2. Compute again. Why does m shrink the multiplier?

14. Closed economy with income tax t = 0.25 on each dollar of GDP and MPC = 0.8 out of disposable income. Compute the effective multiplier using $1/(1 - MPC(1-t))$.

15. Trap: A student applies the closed-economy multiplier of 5 to a $100B stimulus and predicts ΔY = $500B. List two leakages that would reduce this prediction in a real economy.

---

## E) Exchange-Rate Direction (3)

16. The euro appreciates against the dollar. State whether this helps or hurts: (a) US exporters to Europe; (b) European exporters to the US; (c) US tourists in Europe.

17. Use PPP. US prices rise 3% per year, Eurozone prices stable. Predicted dollar movement against the euro?

18. Trap: A student writes "currency appreciation makes exports cheaper abroad." Identify and correct the error with a one-sentence mechanism.

---

## F) Comparative Advantage Trap with PPF (3)

19. Country X can produce 10 units of food OR 5 units of cars per worker. Country Y can produce 8 food OR 8 cars. Each has 100 workers. Which country has CA in food, and what is the terms-of-trade range that supports mutual gains?

20. Trap: Country Z is more productive in BOTH goods than country W. A student concludes Z gains nothing from trade. Refute this and identify the correct condition.

21. With CA established, country X fully specializes. Compute its consumption bundle if it trades 60 units of food for cars at terms 1F : 1C, and compare to autarky.

---

## G) Quantity Theory of Money Applications (4)

22. Money grows 12% per year. Real GDP grows 4%. Velocity is rising 1% per year. Predict inflation per the quantity equation in growth-rate form.

23. Central bank wants to stabilize 2% inflation. Real GDP grows 3%. Velocity is constant. Required money growth?

24. A country's M doubles overnight. Real output is unchanged. V is unchanged. What happens to the price level? Is this consistent with money neutrality?

25. Trap: A student says "if the Fed raises money growth, it will permanently raise real output." State the long-run AD/AS prediction and why this claim is wrong.

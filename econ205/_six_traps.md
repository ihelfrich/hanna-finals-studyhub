## The 6 traps Pérez exams reliably set

::: {.callout-warning}
1. **Real vs. nominal interest rate.** Fisher: $r \approx i - \pi^e$. If the question gives nominal and inflation, almost always wants real (or vice-versa).
2. **AD vs. AS shifts under supply shock.** Negative supply shock = AS left; price up, output down. Don't confuse with demand-side recession.
3. **Crowding out direction.** Expansionary fiscal → higher real rates → private investment falls. *Open* economy adds NX falls via FX appreciation.
4. **Open-economy multiplier formula.** Closed: $1/(1-MPC)$. Open with imports: $1/(1-MPC+m)$ where $m$ = marginal propensity to import. Open with taxes too: $1/(1-MPC(1-t)+m)$.
5. **Exchange rate appreciation vs. depreciation direction.** Higher domestic rates → capital inflow → currency *appreciates* → exports cheaper… wait, no — exports *more expensive abroad*, NX falls.
6. **Comparative advantage with PPF math.** Compute *opportunity cost ratios*, not absolute productivities. The country with lower opportunity cost in good X exports X.
:::

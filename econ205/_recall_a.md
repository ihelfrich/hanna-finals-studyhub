# ECON 205 Final Exam — Active Recall Bank Key

## Section A — Pre-Midterm Refresher

1. As price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus.
2. Movement along D = response to own-price change. Shift = change in non-price determinant (income, prefs, related-good prices, expectations, # consumers).
3. A legal max price set below equilibrium — predicts shortage (QD > QS), non-price rationing, quality decline.
4. $\varepsilon_D = \frac{(Q_2-Q_1)/((Q_2+Q_1)/2)}{(P_2-P_1)/((P_2+P_1)/2)}$
5. Inelastic. Revenue rises when P rises (Q falls proportionally less).
6. $Y = C + I + G + NX$
7. To avoid double-counting; their value is embedded in final goods.
8. Real GDP values current-period quantities at base-year prices, holding prices fixed; nominal uses current prices, so it mixes price and quantity changes.
9. $\text{Deflator}=\frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}}\times 100$
10. $\text{LF}=E+U$; $u=U/\text{LF}$; $\text{LFPR}=\text{LF}/\text{adults}$; $\text{EPOP}=E/\text{adults}$
11. Not working and not searching → out of LF. When unemployed → discouraged, both U and LF fall, u-rate mechanically falls though no one was hired.
12. $S = Y - C - G$
13. $S = I + NX$
14. $Y$ rises by $\sqrt{2}\approx 1.41$. Diminishing marginal returns to capital.
15. R&D subsidies, education/human-capital investment, IP protection, openness to technology diffusion (any two).

## Section B — Money & Inflation

16. $M\cdot V = P\cdot Y$. M = money stock, V = velocity, P = price level, Y = real output.
17. $\%\Delta M \approx \pi$ — money growth in excess of real output growth becomes inflation.
18. Exact: $1+i=(1+r)(1+\pi^e)$. Approx: $i \approx r + \pi^e$.
19. $r \approx 6\% - 3\% = 3\%$.
20. Shoe-leather costs, menu costs, tax distortions on nominal income/capital gains, relative-price confusion, redistribution via fixed nominal contracts (any three).
21. Unexpected inflation: debtors gain (repay in cheaper $); creditors lose.

## Section C — Fluctuations & AD/AS

22. Wealth effect (P↑ → real wealth↓ → C↓); interest-rate effect (P↑ → money demand↑ → r↑ → I↓); exchange-rate / international (P↑ → $ appreciates → NX↓).
23. Sticky wages/prices, menu costs, misperceptions: firms produce more when P↑ but costs lag.
24. In long run, prices, wages, expectations adjust fully; output equals $Y^*$ determined by A, K, L.
25. Short run: P↑ and Y↑ together (move along SRAS).
26. P↑, Y↓, u↑ — stagflation.
27. Simultaneous rising prices and falling output (or rising unemployment).
28. AD↓ → P↓, Y<Y*. Over time wages fall → SRAS shifts right → economy returns to Y* at lower P.
29. A 1 percentage-point change in u-rate corresponds to ~2% opposite-direction change in real GDP gap (course form: $\Delta u \approx -0.5 \cdot$ output-gap growth).
30. Potential output = output when all factors fully employed at natural rate. Determined by A, K, L (Solow long-run growth).

## Section D — Fiscal Policy

31. $1/(1-MPC)$.
32. $-MPC/(1-MPC)$.
33. $1$. Equal G↑ and T↑ leave GDP up by exactly the G change because the spending multiplier exceeds the tax multiplier by 1 in absolute terms.
34. Crowding out: G↑ (deficit-financed) → loanable-funds demand↑ → r↑ → I↓ (and NX↓ via $ appreciation). Partially offsets stimulus.
35. Progressive income tax (revenue falls automatically in recession), unemployment insurance (transfer rises automatically). Both stabilize disposable income without legislative lag.
36. Deficit = annual shortfall (G + Transfers − T). Debt = accumulated past deficits — a stock.
37. Marginal propensity to import (m) is a leakage: each round of induced spending loses some to foreign goods, shrinking the multiplier.
38. When MPC is high, slack is large (recession), monetary policy not offsetting, and crowding out is small (e.g., ZLB).

## Section E — Monetary Policy

39. Open-market operations, discount rate, reserve requirement, interest on reserves (IOR). (QE/forward guidance are unconventional.)
40. Buy bonds → reserves↑ → fed funds rate↓ → market r↓ → I↑, C↑, NX↑ (via $ depreciation) → AD shifts right → P↑, Y↑ short run.
41. $mm = 1/rr$; $M = mm\cdot MB$.
42. ZLB: nominal i cannot fall meaningfully below 0 (cash is alternative). Tools: QE (asset purchases) and forward guidance.
43. $i = r^* + \pi + 0.5(\pi - \pi^*) + 0.5(y - y^*)$.
44. Recognition, decision, implementation, and impact lags total 6–18 months — can stabilize or destabilize depending on timing.
45. Central-bank communication about future policy path to influence current long-term rates.
46. IOR is the rate paid on reserves held at the Fed (sets a floor on fed funds). Discount rate is the rate at which banks borrow directly from the Fed (penalty/ceiling).

## Section F — Financial Markets

47. A bond's coupon is fixed; if market r rises, the present value of those coupons falls — price drops, yield rises.
48. $PV = \sum_t CF_t/(1+r)^t$.
49. Required excess return over the risk-free rate to compensate for default, inflation, liquidity, or term risk.
50. Higher disposable income, expected lower future income, higher r, demographic shift toward middle-age savers (any one).
51. Government deficit → public dis-saving → S shifts left → r↑ → I↓ (crowding out).

## Section G — International Trade

52. CA = lower opportunity cost in producing a good. Absolute advantage = lower input use per unit. CA, not AA, drives gains from trade.
53. Terms of trade must lie strictly between the two countries' opportunity-cost ratios for both to gain.
54. Tariff: domestic P rises by tariff (small country case). Domestic Q rises, imports fall. Areas: producer surplus rectangle (transfer), gov tariff revenue rectangle, two deadweight-loss triangles (production + consumption distortions).
55. Tariff revenue accrues to government; quota rent accrues to whoever holds import licenses (often foreign exporters or domestic importers).

## Section H — International Finance

56. Convention: $E$ = foreign currency per dollar (or vice versa). State explicitly in solution.
57. Exports more expensive abroad → X↓; imports cheaper at home → M↑; NX↓.
58. PPP: $E = P^*/P$ in long run (a basket should cost the same after currency conversion).
59. By construction CA + KA ≈ 0. Trade deficit (CA<0) financed by net foreign capital inflow (KA>0).
60. $S - I = NX$, so if $S<I$, then $NX<0$ — trade deficit financed by capital inflow.

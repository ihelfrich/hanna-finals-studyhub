## Section II: Short Answer

### 21) Hyperinflation
1. $MV=PY$. Persistent rapid M growth with stagnant Y → P explodes. Money is created to finance the deficit (seigniorage).
2. Any two: (a) shoe-leather costs (rapid spending of money to avoid loss), (b) menu costs (constant repricing), (c) breakdown of price signals → misallocation, (d) flight to foreign currency / dollarization.
3. Stabilization typically requires: (i) eliminating monetization of the deficit (fiscal consolidation), (ii) credible commitment from central bank, sometimes via fixed exchange rate or currency board, (iii) institutional reform.

### 22) AD/AS
1. AD shifts left (consumption falls).
2. P↓, Y↓, u↑ (recessionary disinflation).
3. Without policy: persistent slack pushes wages and prices down → SRAS shifts right gradually → economy returns to Y* at lower P.

### 23) Loanable funds
1. Tax cut → public dis-saving → S shifts left.
2. r↑, I↓ (crowding out).
3. r↑ relative to abroad → capital inflow → $ appreciates → X↓, M↑ → NX↓.

### 24) Bond pricing
1. $1000/952.38 - 1 = 0.05$ → yield = 5%.
2. New price = $1000/1.07 = $934.58.
3. Bond price and yield are inversely related.

### 25) Tariff vs quota
- Tariff: tax on imports. Government collects tariff revenue. Domestic producers gain, consumers lose, two DWL triangles (production + consumption distortions).
- Quota: quantitative limit. Rents accrue to license holders (often foreign exporters via voluntary export restraints, or domestic importers). Same DWL triangles, but the "tariff revenue" rectangle becomes private rent rather than government revenue.
- Welfare: tariff dominates quota when government revenue is preferred to private rent transfer abroad.

## Section III: Quantitative Problems

### 26) Open-economy multiplier
1. Closed: $1/(1-0.7)=3.33$. Open: $1/(1-0.7+0.15)=1/0.45 = 2.22$.
2. Gap = 22,000 - 24,000 = -2,000. $\Delta G = 2000/2.22 = \$900$.
3. Tax multiplier (open) = $-MPC/(1-MPC+m) = -0.7/0.45 = -1.556$. $\Delta T = 2000/(-1.556) = -1285.7$ → tax cut of ~$1,286.
4. Effective multiplier $= 2.22 \times 0.75 = 1.667$. $\Delta G = 2000/1.667 = \$1,200$.

### 27) Inflation
1. $\pi = (294-280)/280 = 5.0\%$.
2. Nominal wage growth = $1500/50000 = 3.0\%$. Real wage falls (3% < 5%). Real wage change ≈ $3-5 = -2\%$. Exact: $(1.03/1.05)-1 = -1.90\%$.
3. Approx: $r \approx 6 - 5 = 1\%$. Exact: $1.06/1.05 - 1 = 0.952\%$.

### 28) PPF & CA
1. Autarky max:
   - A: 25 cars OR 20 computers
   - B: 16.67 cars OR 33.33 computers
2. Opportunity cost:
   - A: 1 car costs 4/5 = 0.8 computers; 1 computer costs 5/4 = 1.25 cars
   - B: 1 car costs 6/3 = 2 computers; 1 computer costs 3/6 = 0.5 cars
3. A has CA in cars (0.8 < 2 computers). B has CA in computers (0.5 < 1.25 cars).
4. Mutually beneficial terms: between 0.8 and 2 computers per car (equivalently, between 0.5 and 1.25 cars per computer).
5. At 1 car = 1 computer: ratio is inside (0.8, 2). Both gain. Country A gives up only 0.8 computers domestically per car, so trading 1 car for 1 computer (a better-than-domestic 1.0) is profitable. Country B gives up 0.5 cars per computer, so trading 1 computer for 1 car (better than 0.5) profits B.

### 29) Monetary transmission
1. (i) Long-term r falls (term-structure relation).
   (ii) Cost of capital falls → I↑.
   (iii) Cost of consumer credit falls, wealth↑ → C↑.
   (iv) $ depreciates → NX↑.
   (v) AD shifts right.
2. Short run: P↑, Y↑, u↓.
3. Long run (at full employment): SRAS shifts left as wages catch up; Y returns to Y*; only P higher. Money is neutral long run.

## Section IV: Long Applied

### 30) Open-economy fiscal stimulus
a) Open multiplier = $1/(1-0.75+0.10) = 1/0.35 = 2.857$. Predicted $\Delta Y = 2.857 \times 300 = \$857$B.

b) Deficit-financed G → S left in loanable funds → r↑ → I↓ partially offsets stimulus (crowding out).

c) Higher r relative to abroad → capital inflow → $ appreciates → X↓, M↑ → NX↓ further reducing the multiplier (open-economy crowding out via exchange-rate channel).

d) Realized ΔY likely SMALLER than (a) prediction because the simple multiplier ignores both crowding-out channels (loanable funds and exchange rate).

e) Larger multiplier is plausible when: (i) economy is at ZLB so the Fed accommodates, no r rise; (ii) substantial slack (high u, low capacity utilization); (iii) high-MPC recipients (low-income transfers); (iv) fixed exchange rate eliminating the appreciation channel; (v) imports are scarce/highly substitutable. (Any two with brief mechanism.)

## 2) High-Yield Concept Map (Post-Midterm)

### Ch. 11 — Money and Inflation
- $MV = PY$ → growth form: $\%\Delta M + \%\Delta V = \pi + \%\Delta Y$
- Fisher: $1+i = (1+r)(1+\pi^e)$; approx $i \approx r + \pi^e$
- Long run: money is neutral; in short run, money matters
- Costs of inflation: shoe-leather, menu, distortions, redistribution
- Hyperinflation: monetization of deficits

### Ch. 12–14 — Fluctuations & AD/AS
- AD slopes down: wealth, interest, exchange-rate effects
- SRAS slopes up: sticky wages/prices
- LRAS vertical at Y*
- Demand shock: P, Y move TOGETHER
- Supply shock: P, Y move OPPOSITE
- Long-run self-correction: SRAS shifts as wages adjust
- Stagflation: classic adverse SRAS shock

### Ch. 15 — Fiscal Policy
- Spending multiplier: $1/(1-MPC)$
- Tax multiplier: $-MPC/(1-MPC)$
- Balanced-budget multiplier: 1
- Open-economy multiplier shrinks with imports
- Crowding out: G↑ → r↑ → I↓
- Automatic stabilizers smooth without legislation

### Ch. 16 — Monetary Policy
- Tools: OMO, IOR, discount rate, reserve requirement
- Expansionary: buy bonds → reserves↑ → fed funds↓ → r↓ → I, C, NX↑ → AD right
- Money multiplier: 1/rr
- ZLB → QE, forward guidance
- Taylor rule: react to π gap and y gap

### Ch. 17 — Financial Markets
- Bond price ↔ yield inverse
- PV: $\sum CF_t/(1+r)^t$
- Loanable funds: deficit shifts S left → r↑ → I↓

### Ch. 18 — International Trade
- CA based on opportunity cost (NOT absolute productivity)
- Mutually beneficial terms lie strictly between the two opp-cost ratios
- Tariff: P↑ domestic, DWL = two triangles, government keeps revenue
- Quota: similar but rents go to license holders

### Ch. 19 — International Finance
- $ appreciates → X↓, M↑, NX↓
- PPP long run: $E = P^*/P$
- $S - I = NX$; CA + KA ≈ 0
- Higher domestic r → capital inflow → currency appreciates → NX↓

## 3) Top 20 Final-Exam Mistakes to Avoid

1. Confusing the Fisher direction (writing i=r-π or worse)
2. Treating supply shocks like demand shocks (P, Y direction)
3. Forgetting that LRAS is vertical → no long-run output effect of AD
4. Using closed-economy multiplier in obviously open-economy problems
5. Forgetting crowding out exists outside ZLB
6. CA based on absolute productivity instead of opportunity cost
7. Reversing the appreciation direction on exports
8. Stating the tax multiplier is positive
9. Forgetting balanced-budget multiplier = 1
10. Mixing $\Delta M$ and $\%\Delta M$ in quantity theory
11. Confusing tariff revenue with quota rent
12. Treating QE as a conventional tool
13. Forgetting bond price ↔ yield inverse
14. Sign errors on NX = S - I
15. Forgetting the IOR floor on fed funds
16. Calling discouraged workers unemployed (pre-midterm carryover)
17. Mixing nominal and real rates when computing real returns
18. Forgetting capital-account/current-account mirror
19. Treating money as neutral in the SHORT run (it isn't)
20. Forgetting to label graphs (axes, equilibrium points, shifts)

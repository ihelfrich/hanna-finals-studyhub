## Part I: Multiple Choice Logic

1.  **Ans: B (Debit RE, Credit Dividends)**
    *   *Logic:* Dividends reduce Retained Earnings (Equity). Since RE has a normal credit balance, we Debit it to decrease it. We Credit Dividends to "empty" the account for the next period.

2.  **Ans: B ($32,000)**
    *   *Derivation:*
        *   Annual Depreciation = $(50,000 - 5,000) / 5 = 9,000$.
        *   Accumulated Depreciation (End of Y2) = $9,000 \times 2 = 18,000$.
        *   Book Value = $Cost - Acc. Dep. = 50,000 - 18,000 = 32,000$.

3.  **Ans: B (Net Income Overstated)**
    *   *Logic:* $COGS = Beg~Inv + Purc - End~Inv$. If $End~Inv$ is too high, $COGS$ is too low. If expenses ($COGS$) are too low, profit (Net Income) is too high.

4.  **Ans: A (Increase)**
    *   *Logic:* If Current Ratio $> 1.0$, decreasing both the numerator and denominator by the same amount (Cash and Accounts Payable) mathematically *increases* the ratio. (Example: $250/100 = 2.5$; subtract 50 from both: $200/50 = 4.0$).

5.  **Ans: B (Deducted from NI)**
    *   *Logic:* The "Gain" is an accounting adjustment included in Net Income but it is not a "cash inflow from operations." To find actual cash flow, we must remove the non-cash gain from the starting Net Income. (The actual cash cash flow of $10k appears in the *Investing* section).

## Part III: Problem B (Incremental Analysis)

### 1. Identify Relevant Costs
*   **Relevant Costs:** Variable Costs ($1,200), Special Port Fee ($500), and Special Price Revenue ($1,500).
*   **Irrelevant Costs:** Allocated Fixed Costs ($800) because they will be paid regardless of the university order.

### 2. Calculation (Total for 5 trips)
*   Incremental Revenue ($1,500 \times 5$): $7,500
*   Incremental Variable Cost ($1,200 \times 5$): ($6,000)
*   **Incremental Contribution Margin:** $1,500
*   Less: One-time Port Fee: ($500)
*   **Incremental Net Income: $1,000**

### 3. Recommendation
**Accept the order.**
**Justification:** The order contributes an additional **$1,000** to the company's bottom line without increasing fixed overhead or displacing regular paying customers. As a "User" of the information, the manager sees that the "Opportunity Cost" of capacity is currently zero, so any positive contribution margin after one-time fees is a net benefit.

*   **Special Orders:** Calculating the minimum price to accept a one-time order without cannibalizing existing sales.

### 2. High-Yield Final Exam "Stumbling Blocks"
Based on USC final exam trends for 410x, these are the conceptual traps to drill:

#### A. Statement of Cash Flows (The "Indirect Method" Trap)
*   **The Drill:** Give them Net Income, and a list of balance sheet changes (Increase in AR, Decrease in AP, Gain on Sale of Land).
*   **The Trap:** Students often treat a "Gain" as a cash inflow (it’s actually a non-cash deduction from Net Income in the Operating section).

#### B. Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) with Multiple Products
*   **The Drill:** Calculate the Weighted-Average Contribution Margin for a company selling two products with different sales mixes.
*   **The Trap:** Calculating break-even for only one product or failing to account for the mix.

#### C. Inventory Valuation Errors
*   **The Drill:** If ending inventory is *understated* in Year 1, what happens to COGS and Net Income in Year 1 and Year 2?
*   **The Logic:** It's a self-correcting error over two years. Year 1 NI is understated; Year 2 NI is overstated.

#### D. Bond Pricing & Interest
*   **The Drill:** If a bond's stated rate is 5% and the market rate is 7%, is it a discount or premium?
*   **The Logic:** "Coupon < Market = Discount." Students must understand that the company is "less attractive," so the bond sells for less than par.

### 4. Strategic Advice for the Client
*   **Don't Memorize Journal Entries:** 410x is a "User" class. Focus on how the *transaction* changes the *Balance Sheet totals*.
*   **Keywords:** Watch for "Accrued" (recorded but not paid) vs. "Prepaid" (paid but not recorded).
*   **The "Why":** For every calculation, ask: "If I'm the manager, how does this number change my decision?"

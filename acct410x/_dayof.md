# ACCT 410x: Final Exam Day-Of Playbook
**2-hour cumulative final | Spring 2026**

---

## 1. Order of Attack

1. **Multiple Choice first (target ~40 minutes).** Skim all 25; answer the easy ones in one pass. Flag any MC that requires a calculation longer than 30 seconds — come back to those.
2. **Applied Calculation Problems second (target ~60 minutes).** These have the biggest point swings. Cash Flow (Indirect) and CVP/Incremental Analysis are likely the two big problems.
3. **Conceptual / short-answer last (target ~15 minutes).** These reward clear thinking, not speed.
4. **Final 5 minutes:** review flagged MC and verify totals on the calculation problems.

*Rule of thumb:* never spend more than 1.5× the average time per question on a stuck item. Move on, come back.

---

## 2. The 60-Second Self-Check (do this on every calculation problem)

Before circling your final number, ask:
1. **Sign check:** Is my answer positive when it should be? (Cash from Ops can be negative — that's allowed. NI overstated/understated — make sure you flipped the right direction.)
2. **Magnitude check:** Is this in the ballpark? (A small company shouldn't have $50M cash flow. Break-even units shouldn't be 3 if fixed costs are $90K.)
3. **Units check:** Did the question ask for units, dollars, or a ratio? Did it ask for *one* product or the *total*?
4. **"What does this mean for a manager?":** If you can't translate the number into a sentence ("the firm needs to sell 1,035 boards to cover fixed costs"), you may have computed the wrong thing.

---

## 3. The Three "If I'm Stuck" Pivot Moves

- **Pivot 1 — Work backward from the balance sheet.** If you can't remember a journal entry, ask: "After this transaction, which balance sheet accounts must move, and in which direction?" Assets = Liabilities + Equity must hold. Reconstruct the entry from the required balance change.
- **Pivot 2 — Plug into the equation, even with placeholders.** For COGS, CVP, or Cash Flow, write the formula skeleton (e.g., `BI + P − EI = COGS`) and fill in what you know. The missing variable is the answer. Don't try to do it in your head.
- **Pivot 3 — Translate the trap into plain English.** "Gain on sale" = "the income statement got a credit that wasn't cash." "EI understated" = "we acted like we had less stuff than we did, so COGS is too high." If you can say what's *really happening*, the number direction follows.

---

## 4. Trap-Area One-Liners (memorize these the morning of)

- **Indirect Cash Flow:** *Subtract gains, add back losses, add back depreciation.* Increase in current asset = subtract; increase in current liability = add.
- **Multi-Product CVP:** *Always weight by sales mix before dividing fixed costs.* Never use a single product's CM when the firm sells more than one.
- **Inventory Errors:** *Year 1 EI error reverses in Year 2.* Cumulative two-year RE is correct, but each individual year's NI is wrong by the error amount, in opposite directions.
- **Bond Pricing:** *Coupon < Market → Discount; Coupon > Market → Premium.* Under effective interest, discount bonds have *rising* expense; premium bonds have *falling* expense.

---

## 5. Time Budget (120 minutes)

| Block | Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| MC pass 1 | 30 | Easy MC, flag hard ones |
| MC pass 2 | 10 | Return to flagged MC |
| Problem A (Cash Flow) | 25 | Read twice, write formula skeleton, fill in, self-check |
| Problem B (CVP / Incremental) | 25 | Identify relevant vs. irrelevant first, then compute |
| Conceptual / short answer | 15 | Clear sentences; "what it means for the manager" |
| Final review | 15 | Verify totals, sign checks, fill any blanks |

---

## 6. The "User Class" Mindset (one final reminder)

ACCT 410x is a **user-oriented** course. When in doubt on a wording question, choose the answer that best describes **what the number means for a decision-maker** — not the most technically detailed journal entry. If two MC answers seem right, pick the one a CFO would say to a board, not the one a bookkeeper would write in the ledger.

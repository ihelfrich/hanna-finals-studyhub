## Executive Summary

Hammad's final is essay-format and tests a single throughline: the Middle East regional order has shifted from an **API-anchored framework** (Arab Peace Initiative, 2002 — Israeli withdrawal as the price of normalization) to an **Abraham Accords framework** (2020 — normalization decoupled from the Palestinian question). The exam wants you to see that this is not just a diplomatic shuffle: it is a *rupture* in the regional structure that re-aligns four actor clusters (Israel, the Gulf monarchies, Iran's "axis of resistance," and the United States as a primacy-not-hegemony actor per Gause) around two interlocking subconflicts — Syrian-Israeli and Gulf-Iranian — that have come to *substitute for* the Israeli-Palestinian conflict as the regional axis. The whole system runs as a **Nash equilibrium "carousel"**: each player's best response, given everyone else's strategy, sustains escalation-without-resolution. Iran is the linkage engine across both subspheres (Hezbollah/Syria westward, Gulf eastward); the API-to-Accords pivot is what allows the carousel to keep turning while the Palestinian question is structurally bracketed.

## High-Yield (memorize cold)

- **Dates:** API = March 28, 2002 (Beirut); Abraham Accords = September 15, 2020; Syria civil war begins 2011, Assad regime collapse 2024 (per study guide framing); UAE/Bahrain 2020, Sudan/Morocco late 2020 → 2021; Egypt-Israel 1979; Jordan-Israel 1994.
- **Four key (unequal) actors in the "peace process":** Israel, US, the Arab state cluster (Gulf monarchies + Egypt/Jordan), the Palestinians (PA/Hamas split since 2007). Hammad emphasizes asymmetry — the US is primacy not hegemony (Gause); Palestinians are the weakest party with no veto.
- **Axis of resistance:** Iran — Syria (Assad regime, pre-2024) — Hezbollah (Lebanon) — plus Iraqi Shia militias and Houthis. Engine = strategic depth + deterrence against Israel/Gulf, not pan-Islamism.
- **Hammad's analytic frames:** Bellin (robustness of authoritarianism / coercive apparatus); Brownlee-Masoud-Reynolds (hereditary executive + oil rents = survival); Mitchell *Carbon Democracy* (oil infrastructure shapes politics; MENA = most-penetrated subsystem); Gause (US has primacy, never hegemony); Diwan-Malik-Atiyas (crony capitalism reduces growth); Sayigh (Egyptian military ≈ 25% of govt spending).
- **Two substitute subconflicts** (Q2): (a) Syrian-Israeli / "northern front" (Iran-Syria-Hezbollah vs. Israel); (b) Gulf security / Iranian-Saudi rivalry. Both *displace* Israeli-Palestinian as the organizing regional axis.

## Five Essay-Bank Thesis Statements (adapt under pressure)

5. *"Iran's regional policy is best read through Mitchell's infrastructural lens and Gause's primacy framework: the 'axis of resistance' is a strategic-depth project, not an ideological export, calibrated against US primacy and Israeli deterrence."*

## Q1: Introduction — essay on rupture and alliance of each actor between Arab-Israeli, Syrian-Israeli, and Gulf security conflicts

**Thesis (one line):** The Middle East regional order has fragmented into three nested theatres (Arab-Israeli, Syrian-Israeli, Gulf security) whose actor alignments rupture along the API-to-Abraham-Accords pivot, with Iran as the linkage engine and the US as a primacy-only outside power.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. Map actor alignments on each axis: Arab-Israeli (Israel + Gulf normalizers vs. Palestinians + axis of resistance, post-2020); Syrian-Israeli (Israel vs. Iran-Syria-Hezbollah, 2011-2024); Gulf security (Saudi-UAE-Bahrain + US vs. Iran + proxies).
2. Mark the rupture: API (2002) framework treated the three axes as *conditionally linked* (no normalization without Palestinian statehood); Abraham Accords (2020) *decoupled* them, allowing Gulf-Israeli alignment against Iran while bracketing Palestine.
3. Show alliance shifts: UAE-Israel security cooperation against Iranian drones/proxies; Saudi-Israeli back-channels around 2023; collapse of Syria as Iranian forward base in 2024 reshapes the northern front.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- Gause: US never had hegemony — only primacy — so it can shape but not impose alignments. The Accords are a US-brokered re-alignment under conditions of declining US commitment.
- Bellin: Gulf coercive apparatus + oil rents make normalization sustainable domestically without democratic buy-in.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Treating the three theatres as independent. Hammad's whole point is they are *linked* by Iran and by the API/Accords pivot. Also: do not write a chronological narrative — write a structural-alignment essay.

## Q2: Identify two subconflicts that substitute for the Israeli-Palestinian conflict

**Thesis (one line):** The Syrian-Israeli conflict (the "northern front" linking Iran-Syria-Hezbollah) and the Gulf security conflict (Iran-Saudi rivalry over the Persian Gulf and Yemen) have functionally substituted for the Israeli-Palestinian conflict as the regional organizing axis.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. Subconflict A — Syrian-Israeli: Israeli airstrikes on Iranian/Hezbollah assets in Syria (2013–2024); Hezbollah-Israel exchanges (2023-24); the front is *between Israel and Iran's forward deployment*, with Palestinians as a secondary issue.
2. Subconflict B — Gulf security: Iran-Saudi proxy war in Yemen (Houthis, since 2015); Iranian drone/missile threats to Gulf shipping; Saudi-Iran rapprochement (Beijing-mediated, 2023) as evidence the subconflict is the regional pivot.
3. Substitution evidence: Abraham Accords + 2023 Saudi-Israeli normalization talks proceeded *without* Palestinian preconditions — the regional diplomatic agenda is now organized around Iran, not Palestine.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- Mitchell, *Carbon Democracy*: oil infrastructure makes the Gulf the most-penetrated subsystem; the Gulf-security axis is materially overdetermined.
- API-to-Accords pivot is the structural mechanism of substitution.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Saying Palestine has been "forgotten." The substitution is *structural-diplomatic*, not normative. Palestinian publics and Arab street opinion remain Palestine-focused; *state* behavior has shifted.

**Citation hooks:**
- Beinin & Hajjar, *Primer*, 2014, on regionalization of the conflict.
- Abraham Accords Declaration, 2020.
- Mitchell, Timothy, *Carbon Democracy*, 2011 (lecture frame).

## Q3: Be able to discuss 2-for-2 — what it means to be a new frame of reference connected to API

**Thesis (one line):** A "new frame of reference" connected to API means a diplomatic architecture that retains API's *land-for-peace* logic but updates the actor set and the conditionality structure — the Accords keep the form (multilateral normalization) while inverting the substance (no Palestinian precondition).

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. API's frame: collective Arab offer, 1967-line withdrawal, Palestinian state with East Jerusalem capital, Resolution 194 refugee solution → normalization.
2. Accords' frame: bilateral Gulf-Israel deals, no territorial precondition, security/economic cooperation as the reward — *and Israel's commitment to "pause" (not cancel) West Bank annexation* as the only Palestinian-related concession.
3. The "2-for-2" reading: two frames (API, Accords) for two subconflicts (Syrian-Israeli, Gulf-security) — each frame allocates regional alignment differently and produces different equilibria.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- This is the central pivot of the whole final. Every other question reads through this rupture.
- Gause: US primacy lets it broker the Accords but cannot enforce API.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Treating API and Accords as compatible. They are *substitutes*, not complements. The Accords' existence presupposes the failure (or shelving) of API.

**Citation hooks:**
- Arab Peace Initiative, 2002.
- Abraham Accords Declaration, 2020.
- UN Security Council Resolution 242, 1967 (the underlying land-for-peace formula both frames inherit).

## Q4: Arab Peace Initiative

**Thesis (one line):** The API (March 28, 2002) was the first and only collective Arab offer of full normalization conditioned on Israeli withdrawal to 1967 lines, a Palestinian state, and a "just solution" to the refugee question per Resolution 194 — and Israel's non-response combined with subsequent Gulf bilateralism rendered it diplomatically dormant by 2020.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. Origins: Saudi Crown Prince Abdullah's initiative, adopted unanimously at the Beirut Arab League Summit, March 2002, in the middle of the Second Intifada.
2. Three substantive demands: (a) full Israeli withdrawal from territories occupied since June 1967, (b) Palestinian state with East Jerusalem capital, (c) "just solution" to refugees per UNGA 194.
3. Fate: Israel never formally responded; reaffirmed at 2007 Riyadh Summit; structurally bypassed by Abraham Accords in 2020. Still formally "on the table" but no enforcement coalition.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- API is the pre-rupture baseline for every Accords-related question. It encodes the *linkage* logic (no normalization without Palestine) that the Accords break.
- The "just solution per 194" language is deliberately ambiguous on right-of-return, mirroring 242's "territories" ambiguity.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Calling API a "failure." It set the regional standard for two decades; its bypass is the analytical event, not its content.

**Citation hooks:**
- Arab League Summit, *Arab Peace Initiative (Beirut Declaration)*, March 28, 2002.
- UNGA Resolution 194 (1948) — refugee return formula it inherits.
- UNSC Resolution 242 (1967) — land-for-peace base.

## Q5: Syrian conflict — sources of Syrian-Iranian alliance and how it connects the two subregional conflicts

**Thesis (one line):** The Syrian-Iranian alliance (formalized after 1979) rests on three host-side pillars — Alawite minority regime survival, anti-US/anti-Israel deterrence, and forward-basing for Hezbollah — and it operates as the connective tissue between the Syrian-Israeli northern front and the Gulf-security axis.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. Host-perspective sources: Assad regime (Alawite minority ~12% of population) needs external backing to survive Sunni-majority politics — Iranian IRGC, Quds Force, and Hezbollah ground support during 2011–present civil war kept the regime alive until 2024.
2. Strategic logic: Syria is Iran's *land bridge* to Hezbollah in Lebanon — weapons transfers, training, strategic depth against Israel. Without Syria, Iranian regional projection collapses to Iraq and Yemen.
3. Cross-conflict linkage: the Syrian alliance lets Iran pressure Israel from the north (Hezbollah rockets, Golan front) *and* destabilize the Gulf (Iraqi militias, Houthi support) simultaneously — one alliance, two theatres.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- Bellin: Assad's coercive apparatus (mukhabarat, military) explains regime durability through 2011 uprising; external rents (Iranian/Russian) substitute for oil rents.
- Brownlee-Masoud-Reynolds: hereditary executive (Hafez → Bashar) + minority coercive apparatus = survival prediction met until exogenous shock.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Framing the alliance as Shia-sectarian. Alawites are heterodox, not Twelver Shia; the alliance is *strategic*, not theological. Sectarianism is a discursive overlay.

**Citation hooks:**
- Bellin, "Robustness of Authoritarianism" (lecture frame; specific cite to confirm).
- Brownlee, Masoud & Reynolds, *The Arab Spring: Pathways of Repression and Reform* (2015) — frame to confirm.

## Q6: How the collapse of the Syrian regime will affect regional politics

**Thesis (one line):** Assad's collapse (2024 per study guide) ruptures Iran's land bridge to Hezbollah, weakens the northern front against Israel, opens a contestation space for Turkey and Gulf states, and accelerates the Accords-side regional realignment — but it does *not* resolve the Israeli-Palestinian core.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. Iran-side cost: loss of forward-basing, weapons-transit corridor, and IRGC infrastructure. Hezbollah's strategic depth shrinks; Israeli deterrence calculus shifts.
2. Sunni-side opportunity: Turkey (HTS-linked factions), Saudi Arabia, UAE compete for influence over post-Assad Syria; reconstruction dollars become a leverage tool.
3. Regional alignment: weakened axis of resistance reduces the deterrent value of Iranian regional projection, increasing Gulf willingness to formalize Israeli alignment (Saudi normalization track revived).

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- Bellin: Assad's coercive apparatus eventually exhausted by attrition + external sponsor strain — robustness has limits.
- Mitchell: Syrian oil/pipeline geography (Deir ez-Zor, transit routes) becomes a contestation prize.
- Gause: US primacy shapes but does not direct the post-Assad transition; multiple regional players move into the vacuum.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Treating regime collapse as the end of Iranian regional power. Iran retains Iraq, Yemen, nuclear-program leverage, and domestic mobilization capacity. The collapse weakens but does not eliminate the axis.

## Q7: Impact of normalization on the larger Israeli-Palestinian conflict

**Thesis (one line):** Abraham Accords normalization *structurally weakens Palestinian leverage* by removing Arab-state conditionality, while *strengthening Israeli bargaining power* across all final-status issues — accelerating de facto annexation dynamics in the West Bank without producing a final settlement.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. Pre-Accords baseline: API conditioned normalization on Palestinian statehood; Palestinians had a regional veto via the Arab League framework.
2. Post-Accords reality: UAE/Bahrain/Morocco/Sudan normalized without Palestinian sign-off; Israel paused (did not cancel) West Bank annexation; settlement expansion continued through 2020-2024.
3. Knock-on effects: PA's diplomatic relevance declined; Hamas's "armed resistance" frame gained relative legitimacy in Palestinian street opinion (October 7, 2023, partly read as a response to normalization momentum); Saudi track stalled but not killed.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- Diwan-Malik-Atiyas: Gulf normalization is partly an *economic* play (Israeli tech, defense, water) — crony capitalism's transnational extension. Palestinians have no comparable economic offer.
- The Accords confirm Gause's primacy thesis: US could broker normalization but could not enforce a Palestinian-state outcome.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Saying the Accords "killed" the two-state solution. They *bracketed* it. Two-state remains the formal international position; what changed is the regional incentive structure.

**Citation hooks:**
- Abraham Accords Declaration, 2020.
- Beinin & Hajjar, *Primer*, on settlement expansion through peace-process phases.
- B'Tselem, "The Settlements: Background," 2019.
- ICJ Wall Advisory Opinion, 2004.

## Q8: Impact on both sides

**Thesis (one line):** Normalization yields asymmetric gains — Israel secures regional legitimacy + security cooperation + economic access without territorial concession; the Palestinians lose their structural Arab-state veto and face accelerated facts-on-the-ground in the West Bank — making the equilibrium less stable, not more.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. Israeli side: diplomatic gains (Gulf airspace, embassies, defense cooperation against Iran), economic gains (Emirati/Saudi capital, tech ties), strategic gains (Iran-deterrence coalition). Cost: domestic far-right pressure to annex remains.
2. Palestinian side: PA fiscal/political weakening (Gulf donor flows redirected); Hamas's relative legitimacy rises; settler violence and West Bank settlement expansion accelerate (2021-2024); humanitarian situation in Gaza deteriorates pre- and post-October 7.
3. Equilibrium effect: removing Arab-state mediation removes a *pressure-release valve*. The October 7, 2023 attack and subsequent Gaza war can be read as a violent re-imposition of the Palestinian question on a regional agenda that had bracketed it.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- Sayigh: Israeli military-economic complex parallels Egyptian (~25% of Egyptian govt spending) — institutional incentives sustain occupation regardless of diplomatic frame.
- Bellin: PA's *weak* coercive apparatus (compared to Israeli or Hamas) explains its inability to capture political legitimacy.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Treating "both sides" symmetrically. The asymmetry is the analytical point. Quantify it.

**Citation hooks:**
- Abraham Accords Declaration, 2020.
- Hamas Political Document, 2017.
- OCHA Gaza Situation Report No. 68, February 20, 2026.
- Sayigh, Yezid, on Egyptian military economy (lecture frame; specific Carnegie/SOAS paper to confirm).

## Q9: Abraham Accords / normalization — what does it do to Israeli occupation and authoritarianism in Morocco, Sudan, UAE, Saudi Arabia?

**Thesis (one line):** Normalization simultaneously *entrenches Israeli occupation* (by removing Arab-state conditionality) and *legitimizes Arab authoritarianism* (by trading external recognition + arms transfers for domestic acquiescence) — the two outcomes are causally linked, not coincidental.

**Three evidentiary moves:**
1. **Morocco (Dec 2020):** Normalization paired with US recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara — authoritarian regime gained territorial-legitimacy boost. Bellin frame: monarchy's coercive apparatus reinforced.
2. **Sudan (Jan 2021):** Normalization tied to removal from US State Sponsors of Terrorism list and IMF/debt relief. Transitional government weakened domestically; military coup followed in October 2021. Brownlee-Masoud-Reynolds: military-as-coercive-apparatus + external rent = regime survival.
3. **UAE / Saudi:** Hereditary executive + oil rents (Brownlee et al.) → Gulf regimes do not need domestic buy-in for normalization. Israeli surveillance tech (Pegasus, etc.) directly strengthens authoritarian coercive capacity. Saudi 2023-24 normalization talks stalled by October 7 but track remains live.

**Tie-back to Hammad's frames:**
- Bellin (robustness of authoritarianism): coercive apparatus + external rents predict regime durability.
- Brownlee-Masoud-Reynolds: hereditary executive + oil rents = highest survival probability — explains why Gulf monarchies normalize without domestic cost.
- Diwan-Malik-Atiyas: crony capitalism reduces aggregate growth — Gulf-Israel tech/defense ties are a *crony* extension, not a developmental one.

**Trap to avoid:**
- Treating each normalization as a separate bilateral. The pattern is *structural*: each deal trades Palestinian leverage + arms-transfer asymmetry for authoritarian consolidation. Specify the deal-by-deal trade.
